(almost) all of us know, and would agree that hatred is bad. its been the central theme of basically every movie, book, and song that we've consumed since birth, society ingrains that in us throughout our lives. however, hate, due being a biological necessity, is practically impossible to completely detatch ourselves from.
unless you somehow reach monkhood or are an exceptionally loving person, we all have hate embedded within us. we're all aware that it can, at the very least be controlled and reduced, but that requires effort and real self reflection

so we choose the easier path: justifying it

the issue here is that there really are valid reasons to hate, thats why (at least i think) we ended up developing it. for example you should hate nazis. in fact not hating them would be a moral pitfall on your part. and that's what makes identity politics even worth discussing: it's not as simple as always choosing love over hatred

believing that you've been dealt a shitty hand at life is a very comforting thought. it absolves you of your incompetence and flaws, and gives a perfect punching bag to dump all your failures on

im not gonna push the dumbass centrist take of "both sides bad 🥺🥺". there are real minorities in this world who face disadvantages due to their identity every single day. there's no denying that

i just think that the real test of being progressive should be recognizing the societal structures that affect the entirety of society, rather than finding the ones that disadvantage you, and clinging onto them as a shield to justify your flaws (hatred in this case)

## here comes my hottest take:

if youre only ever concerned with prejudices that you face, as a person, or an individual level, you are no better than a conservative. i said it, and i really mean it. your views happen to be more valid than theirs because you happen to be a minority. if you were born on the other side of the ladder, with those same privileges, there's absolutely no chance you'd ever speak out or care about the same issues you currently face

by far the biggest offenders ive seen of this are straight women. so many of them will self identify as hardcore feminists, which I DONT HAVE AN ISSUE WITH. I'M ONE TOO. and every time one of the stupid gender war discourse points goes viral, they'll be the first to speak up and give their take on it. ask them about their beliefs on other sensitive societal issue however, and youll see the most bigoted, spiteful, and regressive world views pop up

i just dont think there's anything commendable or worth lauding in that? of course you'd be more incentivized to fight back against misogyny as a woman; it directly affects you. but when that misogyny turns into homophobia, suddenly it becomes a subject of ridicule. how is that progressive at all

this also holds true the other way too. any gay man who doesnt concern himself with misogyny is straight up a dumbass

i also have another opinion that im not 100% sure about, but ive vaguely had for years now: yes, advocating for human rights is amazing. we absolutely need it, and it keeps our society going. but people who use that as the only criteria for your value as a person/ of character puzzle me. yes capitalism sucks, and your political inclination obviously reflects on you. however, acting like
its the end-all-be-all of personal worth is pretty ridiculous to me

i think i have a very apt example for this. lets consider these two people:

1. A very successful, but conservative doctor

2. A very vocal activist with nothing else going for them at all

in the traditional setting, obv the former would be regarded much higher on the social hierarchy than the latter.
however, in liberal circles, ive seen a very funny shift in perspective

not only is the latter naturally revered more for their world views, but, in a sense, they are also given credit for "defying capitalism"

this just,,, makes no sense to me? capitalism is very hellish and flawed in many ways, but being a ambitionless bum isnt praise worthy because youre defying the system lmao

anyways, getting back to the point

the conservative doctor, via their work, saves and improves lives every single day. their personal beliefs may be flawed, and theres no excusing or justifying that, but its very very hard to make a convincing argument that theyve had less positive impact in the world than the acitvist. the activist might be very empathatic and open minded, but they lack drive and ambition, which are also very defining factors of one's character. which one takes priority is a seperate and more intricate debate, but you can't really deny that it's important

and this is where a tonnnn of my very woke (i hate this term but its the most fitting here) friends start to lose me. there's (for good reason) a ton of focus on empathy and openness, but, so many of them end up having no real concrete goals or ambitions in the process. this isn't even me trying to one up them, i dont think im anything special in this regard either, but i do want to point out that in the search for inclusivity and comfort; its very easy to lose the spark of drive and hard work within us. accepting our failures is a double edged sword: it negates self resentment, but it leads to us being lax and lazy

okay lets get back to the original argument at hand. hopefully everything ive said so far has laid the groundwork for the upcoming points

everyone craves validation. validation for their successes, validation for their hardships, validation that theyre enough, valdation that theyre a good person. we all, to different levels, crave it

when people feel like their efforts or their hardships are being minimized due to factors outside of their control, they arent exactly pleased about it. that's why, i think, a huge demographic of men vehemently oppose the acknowledgement of the patriarchy. it's not like they set the system up, and they, in their day to day life dont realize the ways it benefits them, because to them, it the norm. that's just how the world is

this constant tug of war between different demographics to have their success validated, and conversely, to have their hardships acknowledged leads to a really vicious and toxic breeding ground for mutual resentment.
