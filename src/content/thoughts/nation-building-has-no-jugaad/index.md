---
title: 'Nation-Building Has No "Jugaad"'
description: 'on the myth of a unified past, the rot of jugaad morality, and why improvisation cannot replace institutional excellence in nation-building'
date: 2026-11-06
tags: ['politics', 'culture', 'philosophy']
---

India is a stillborn baby. Not born but not dead- somewhere in limbo, a ghastly state between birth and death. Buildings half demolished - or half constructed, who knows? This nation is an absurdity, one half (less than half, actually) steeped in elitist privilege and the other half happy with mediocrity and “settling”. The Indian man lacks the Promethean vision, the flair of Marutis and Varunas, content with being a blessed and shackled cow of Gopala. Maybe this is the price we pay as a nation…

India may have a rich past but it does not have a rich Past. What is a Past: a common set of myth, legends, heroes, tales, stories, fiction, overarchingly expressed through the common threads of culture. Not only the historical experiences of a society (the past, with a small ‘p’) but the culture that mediates between it and the current populace.

Many countries trap their Pasts in this and many such ways- the entire project of the European nation-state is, in a way, predicated to trap for a band of people their own Past, to inculcate belief in this ideal glorious Past to direct towards their work on the future. A good Past makes the people happy, productive and tied unto each other. A bad Past inferiorises the population; the non-existence of one makes them confused, and worse, hopeless.

---

India’s problem is the latter one rather than the former (as it is commonly mistaken). We are told that we have been inferiorised by the West, hence our lack of great success in the modern world. If we could *just* let go of this inferiority complex, they say, we would be a superpower. This is an altogether myopic view of what our past has been- for the most part, regional and wildly differentiated. Our heroes and myths differ from family to family, village to village, from tongue to tongue. What our conservatives and right wings live out is a hyperreality of Hinduism- an ugly illogical hodge-podge of various regional instances (not coincidentally deriving from North India). They attempt to construct and impose upon the people these nonexistent past, people whose mothers and grandmothers narrated stories quite opposite from their simple, myopic ‘Hinduism’.

Instead of shoving a non-existent image down the throats of the people, consider a country that has persisted and exemplified the pastlessness of modernity: America. America is truly a land which has no Past- it has no origin story, very little founding myth. The Indian reservations that lay scattered about are just its museums, showing a past most of them do not care about (whilst becoming casino land for many). It has succeeded because it was founded on an ethos- of modernity, work, of following the American dream to the depths of hell and back. As Baudrillard so presciently commented, European ideas and movements bled across the Atlantic and became things that were done- not merely thought; but transformed into action![^1]

Their Cold-War rivals, the Soviets are another great example of this dedication to work as a form of self-sacrifice. One merely has to witness the *art* that was Soviet propaganda, to find rejoice and merriment in dreary factory work. Regardless of which of the thousand ethnicities one was from, one valued work ethic as long as they were in USSR. This is what led to many greatest scientists, mathematicians, chess players to all originate from Soviet countries: a robust system that encouraged education for education’s sake![^2]

These exemplify how one rules a country with no past - through an emergent, unified Ethos.[^3]

In fact, a nation develops its ethos organically as a result of continued interaction and intermingling. In lands with no past, it develops to an extent that it consumes everything, as if the only way to communicate with a distant stranger (but someone from your nation) was the common *ethik* and manner. What is India’s ethos, then? One can say India is friendly to guests, but that is not really true- India is actually a low-trust society. Indeed, handling people from so many backgrounds, many of them are *intrinsically* (in your view) untrustworthy leads to constant paranoia about being cheated- a gaping void of trust remains. The lack of proper common *expectations*[^4] sets this up. Perhaps the prospect of a Past or ethos is integral only as far as it strengthens interactions, setting up greater national cohesion (in the form of trust and solidarity).

The nation our founders imagined was an India preserving this great internal differentiation, our rich cultures, our thousand languages, all coexisting together like a big happy family. A people without a Past, living together in liberal harmony and peace - a fine ethos to begin with, but one that quickly proved unsustainable due to the tensions within India itself. Indeed, binding an ethos to the cultural tensions that lie within India is a risky structure, prone to degradation. What has Nehru’s India become - a stack of territories upon territories upon territories in a never ending always-entangled mess. You try to pull at a ‘caste’ string and everything else comes along with it.

The answer indeed is something else: *jugaad*- shortcuts, clever sleights of hand, simple workarounds. This is why Indians crowd on wheelchairs at airports, why they quietly slip a 500₹ rupee note inside their documents, and why they all also resonantly hate each other- who would not hate every other person if one was at a constant risk of being swindled by some clever trick? A *negative feedback loop* ensues, and all that remains of work ethic and patient application/dedication is burnt away. Tangentially, it is no wonder that India became a greater service economy than an industrial workforce: the former is where efficiency and corporatism is prioritised to the latter’s principle of work, pain and self-sacrifice.

There are situations where the ingenuity of *jugaad* is important, even necessary. But improvisation alone cannot build robust institutions.

To rattle off a few examples of the prevailing ethos’s inability to build robust institutions: Our Silicon Valley is dominated by a brokerage app’s owner, all operated like a family business. Jio’s (a service provided by one of our richest families) market cap is supposedly second largest, whose owners personally blow the government in charge to get favourable contracts (and still has been slipping out of control). It is no wonder that our version of socialism devolved into cronyism- the distasteful “License Raj”. It is no wonder our conservatives became cronies in the process of fighting cronies, slipping out favours for their brethren as if whole of India is their village estate. It is no wonder that any form of politics which touches this nation devolves into this disgusting mix of despotism, nepotism, and money grubbing. It is an illustration of my previous arguments that Indian Communists are suspiciously immune (or at least were) to this sort of influence[^5].

Our culture has in it an inescapable rot, the rot of *jugaad* morality, the rot of slavishness. The youth must cull this rot and the damage it has done- by cultivating an ethos of work, of doing better than oneself, of transcending oneself, of übermensch/’*maurutya’*. There shall be no slogans about what political party is right- instead be sensible discourse and argumentation, explanation not persuasion[^6]. The only sloganeering will be “transcend yourself”. Dare to walk the tightrope, dare to fail, dare to move out of mediocrity. If forged in a culture that values excellence, competence, and intellectual engagement, our youth would be able to set the example of the world. We already know how dedicated our students can be- why then make them lurch towards mediocrity, when they can instead chase excellence. India is much more poised to execute this correctly, as analysts continue to point out- it is only our desire for a simple workaround that has prevented us.

**Nation-building has no *jugaad*.**

[^1]: This is based on a fragment of “America” by Jean Baudrillard that I remember. One might argue that America does have a past (Founding Fathers, cherry tree, etc) - but most of America’s founding myth rests on ***prospective values*** (persistence, work, *pulling yourself up from your bootstraps*) rather than ***historical continuity***.

[^2]: Communist countries are not *literally* pastless, but their fundamental organisational principles have been not that of historical continuity with the previous regimes (indeed that would be traitorous for them), but emphasis on Communist *values* and figures.

[^3]: Even now the distinction does not seem to me to be fundamentally *exclusive*. As in, a thing can be both Ethos and Past, and continuously vary from one category to another. I think, at the end, it rests on how ‘*value-loaded*’ and ‘*history-loaded*’ the particular ‘myths’ are. Currently, this mode seems sufficient.

[^4]: Perhaps the importance of a common Past or Ethos lies less in the content of the myths themselves than in their capacity to establish shared expectations that provide ‘doorknobs’ for meaningful interaction, as well as priors that reasonably inform co-operating under uncertainty. This can be seen as a quasi-Prisoner’s dilemma- but I digress.

[^5]: See Ramachandra Guha, “*The Past and Future of the Indian Left*” for more specifics (and curious history!).

[^6]: The activities of the Contrarian Club in Bengaluru are interesting in this regard.
